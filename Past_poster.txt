TITLE

Context-Aware Kinase-Substrate Association Prediction in Liver Cancer Using a Heterogeneous Graph Network with Spectral Embeddings

---
ABSTRACT

Protein phosphorylation regulates cellular signaling and cancer progression, yet more than 90% of experimentally identified phosphosites lack annotation of their upstream kinases. Existing computational approaches for kinase-substrate association (KSA) prediction are biased toward well-studied kinases, a phenomenon described as the streetlight effect. This imbalance limits discovery of understudied kinases that may play critical roles in disease.

We developed a large-scale heterogeneous protein-phosphosite network integrating biochemical, pathway, interaction, and evolutionary evidence, extended with liver cancer-specific phosphoproteomic correlations. A leave-one-out (LOO) evaluation framework across 420 candidate kinases and 6,909 multi-kinase sites demonstrates that spectral graph embeddings reduce median prediction rank by 57% relative to random-walk embeddings. Analysis of network-dependent rank shifts identifies a necroptosis and innate-immune kinase cluster (RIPK1, RIPK3, MLKL, IRAK4) that gains predicted relevance specifically under the liver cancer correlation network. Kinase-substrate enrichment analysis (KSEA) provides orthogonal validation, identifying 17 significantly active kinases in liver cancer that are distinct from LOO top-ranked kinases, confirming the complementary nature of the two approaches.

---
HYPOTHESIS

Our hypothesis is that integrating heterogeneous biological data within a network framework, combined with stratified modeling and spectral embeddings, can mitigate bias toward well-studied kinases and enable context-aware identification of disease-relevant kinases in liver cancer.

- Annotation imbalance in phosphorylation data produces structural bias toward well-studied kinases.
- Stratifying kinases by annotation density enables fairer prioritization of understudied kinases.
- Integration of liver cancer-specific molecular correlations shifts kinase prioritization toward disease-relevant biology.

---
METHODS

Heterogeneous Network Graph Representation

The heterogeneous graph contains two node types: proteins and phosphosites. Kinases and phosphatases are represented as proteins; their functional roles are encoded in edge types. This design preserves biological identity and ensures that all interactions involving a protein contribute to a unified network representation.

Edge types include:
- Protein to Site: phosphorylates / dephosphorylates
- Protein to Site: has_site
- Protein to Protein: shared pathway and high-confidence interaction
- Site to Site: shared pathway and co-evolution
- Site to Site: liver cancer phosphoproteomic Pearson correlation (positive and negative, 80th percentile threshold)

Context-Specific Liver Cancer Integration

Pearson correlations were computed from liver cancer phosphoproteomic data separately for normal samples, tumor samples, and tumor-to-control fold-change ratios. Edges above the 80th percentile threshold were incorporated, producing three liver-specific graph variants (Control, Cancer, Liver FC) alongside a Generic baseline without correlation edges.

Prediction and Evaluation

KSA prediction was evaluated using a leave-one-out framework. For each multi-kinase phosphosite, one known kinase was held out and ranked among 420 candidate kinases. Graph embeddings were computed on a PhosphoSitePlus-stripped graph to prevent leakage of kinase-substrate labels. Two embedding strategies were compared: node2vec (random-walk) and spectral (Laplacian eigenvectors). Logistic regression was trained on all non-held-out KSA edges and scored each candidate at the test site. The adjusted rank metric removes co-kinases from the candidate list before ranking to prevent artificial inflation by unlabeled true positives. Kinases were stratified into three annotation categories: poor (5 or fewer substrates), average (5 to 20), and rich (more than 20).

Kinase Activity Enrichment Analysis

KSEA was applied to the liver FC dataset using the formula from Wiredja et al. (2017): score = (mean substrate FC minus global mean FC) times sqrt(n substrates), divided by the global FC standard deviation. Significance was assessed by permutation testing with Benjamini-Hochberg correction (64 scoreable kinases with 3 or more detected substrates).

---
RESULTS

LOO Prediction Performance

Node2vec embeddings show improved prediction when disease-specific correlations are included (median adjusted rank 165-174) compared to the generic network (rank 188), confirming that liver cancer phosphoproteomic data contributes meaningful signal. Spectral embeddings further amplify this improvement, achieving median ranks of 74-79 across all four networks regardless of correlation type, representing a 57% reduction relative to node2vec. The topological structure captured by spectral decomposition of the graph Laplacian encodes biological context more effectively than random-walk approaches, and node2vec results demonstrate that the improvement is not exclusive to spectral embedding.

Figure 3.

Median adjusted kinase rank across 6,909 multi-kinase leave-one-out trials. Node2vec and spectral embeddings are compared across four network conditions (Generic, Control, Cancer, Liver FC). Lower rank indicates better prediction performance among 420 candidate kinases. Dashed line indicates the expected random baseline (rank 210).

Network-Dependent Rank Shifts in Liver Cancer

Delta analysis was performed by computing the difference in mean kinase rank between each disease network and the Control network under spectral embedding. Kinases showing the most negative delta in both Cancer-vs-Control and Liver FC-vs-Control comparisons include RIPK1, RIPK3, and MLKL (core necroptosis pathway kinases), IRAK4 (TLR and IL-1R innate-immune signaling), and TRPM7. These kinases do not appear among LOO top-1 predictions due to hub-kinase dominance, underscoring the value of delta analysis for extracting disease-relevant signals that are otherwise obscured.

Figure 4.

Delta heatmap under spectral embedding: mean kinase rank shift versus Control for Cancer and Liver FC networks. Negative values indicate improved predicted relevance under the disease network. Analysis restricted to informative kinases (rank standard deviation greater than 1.0 across networks). Necroptosis and innate-immune cluster kinases are annotated.

Kinase Activity Enrichment in Liver Cancer

KSEA using known PhosphoSitePlus substrates identified 17 of 64 scoreable kinases as significantly enriched in liver cancer (adj. p < 0.05). Top up-regulated kinases include MAPK12, CDKL5, and MAPK13. Top down-regulated kinases include PRKACA, PRKD1, PRKCD, and RPS6KA1. None of these kinases appear in LOO top-1 predictions, where hub kinases dominate. This divergence confirms that LOO ranking and KSEA capture complementary dimensions of kinase biology: predictive association versus substrate activity enrichment. The two methods together provide a more complete picture of kinase dysregulation in liver cancer than either approach alone.

Figure 5.

Kinase activity enrichment scores for liver cancer. Horizontal bars represent KSEA scores for the 17 significantly enriched kinases (adj. p < 0.05, Benjamini-Hochberg correction). Orange: substrate set is hyper-phosphorylated in tumor relative to control. Blue: substrate set is hypo-phosphorylated in tumor relative to control. Significance levels: * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

---
CONCLUSIONS

This study extends a heterogeneous KSA prediction framework with liver cancer-specific molecular correlations and rigorous leave-one-out evaluation across 420 candidate kinases. Spectral graph embeddings substantially outperform random-walk embeddings, reducing median adjusted rank by 57% across all network conditions. Delta analysis under spectral embedding identifies a biologically coherent kinase cluster (RIPK1, RIPK3, MLKL, IRAK4, TRPM7) that gains predicted relevance in liver cancer relative to healthy control. KSEA provides orthogonal validation of kinase activity disruption, identifying a distinct set of significantly active kinases not captured by LOO ranking. Together, these results demonstrate that heterogeneous graph integration with context-specific molecular data and spectral embeddings enables discovery of disease-relevant kinase associations beyond the reach of annotation-biased approaches.
